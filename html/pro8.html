Artificial Intelligence

A field guide, condensed from Wikipedia
Goals How it works Applications Risks & ethics History Future

Artificial intelligence is the capability of computational systems to perform tasks normally associated with human thought — learning, reasoning, perceiving, deciding. It's less a single technology than a shifting label: once a technique works reliably, it usually stops being called "AI" and starts being called software. This page walks through what the field is trying to do, how today's systems do it, where they're already embedded in daily life, and the open arguments about where all of this leads.

01What AI is trying to do

Research in the field has always circled the same handful of capabilities. No single system does all of them well — most narrow that focus deliberately.

Reasoning

Drawing conclusions from evidence — from step-by-step logic to the fast, intuitive judgments most human problem-solving actually relies on.

Knowledge

Representing facts and relationships about the world in a form a program can use and act on.

Planning

Choosing actions toward a goal, often under uncertainty about what will happen next.

Learning

Improving performance from data or experience, rather than being explicitly programmed for every case.

Language

Reading, writing, and holding a conversation — the domain where large language models now dominate.

Perception

Making sense of sensory input: images, speech, video, and other raw signals.

A more ambitious goal sits above all of these: artificial general intelligence, a system that could match human versatility across nearly any cognitive task, rather than excelling at one narrow slice of it.

02How today's systems get there

Modern AI is dominated by machine learning, and within that, by deep learning — neural networks with many layers, trained on large amounts of data rather than hand-written rules.

Neural networks & deep learning

A network of simple nodes, loosely modeled on neurons, learns to map inputs to outputs by adjusting internal weights during training. Stack enough layers and the network starts extracting increasingly abstract features on its own — edges, then shapes, then whole concepts, without anyone specifying what those features should be.

Large language models

Trained to predict the next word across enormous bodies of text, these models absorb patterns in language — and, incidentally, a great deal of world knowledge — well enough to hold a conversation, write code, or summarize a document. They remain prone to confidently stating things that are false, a failure mode usually called hallucination.

A neural network can, in principle, learn any function — the hard part is finding the training data and computing power to get there.

Older, still-useful approaches

Symbolic logic, search algorithms, and probabilistic reasoning (Bayesian networks, decision theory) predate the current deep-learning wave and still underpin planning systems, game engines, and anywhere a decision needs to be explainable rather than just accurate.

03Where it already lives

AI stopped being exotic some time ago. It's quietly load-bearing in most of the software people touch daily.

Search & recommendation

Ranking results, surfacing what to watch or buy next, targeting ads.

Assistants & chatbots

Voice assistants, customer-support bots, and general-purpose conversational tools.

Vision

Face and object recognition, photo labeling, autonomous-vehicle perception.

Science & medicine

Protein-structure prediction, faster drug-candidate screening, diagnostic support tools.

Games & simulation

Superhuman play in chess, Go, StarCraft, and poker; realistic non-player behavior.

Mathematics

Tools that search for proofs, counter-examples, and optimized algorithms alongside human mathematicians.

04What people worry about

The concerns split roughly into problems that are already happening and ones that might happen if the technology keeps scaling.

Bias

Systems trained on biased data reproduce and sometimes amplify that bias — in lending, hiring, and criminal-justice risk scoring.

Opacity

Complex models are often not explainable even by their own designers, which is a problem when a decision needs to be justified.

Misinformation

Generative tools make convincing fake text, images, and video cheap to produce at scale.

Jobs

Automation is projected to reshape or eliminate large categories of white-collar and creative work.

Concentration

The compute and data required for frontier models favor a small number of very large companies.

Environment

Training and running large models is power- and water-intensive, straining grids and local supplies.

Existential risk

A minority but prominent group of researchers argue sufficiently powerful, poorly aligned AI could pose a civilization-level threat.

Opinion among AI researchers themselves is genuinely split on how seriously to weigh the last item — some treat it as the field's central problem, others as a distraction from more immediate harms.

05How we got here

1956The field is founded at a Dartmouth workshop; early optimism follows within a decade.
1970s–80sFunding cuts and unmet promises produce the first "AI winter."
Late 1980sA second winter follows the collapse of the expert-systems market.
2012GPU-trained deep neural networks start beating older methods on benchmark tasks, reviving the field.
2017The transformer architecture arrives, setting up the current generation of language models.
Nov 2022ChatGPT launches and becomes the fastest-growing consumer application in history.
2024–26Reasoning models, math and coding gains, and mounting scrutiny over energy use, copyright, and labor impact.

06Open questions

Whether progress keeps compounding or plateaus is unresolved. Skeptics point out that every technology so far has followed an S-curve rather than an endless climb; others argue this one is different because the systems can, in principle, help improve themselves. Philosophers remain divided on a separate question entirely — whether any of this amounts to genuine understanding, or a very convincing simulation of it. Nobody currently has a way to test the difference from the outside.
